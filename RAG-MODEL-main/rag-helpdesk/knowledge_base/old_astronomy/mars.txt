MARS

1. OVERVIEW
   Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun.
   Mars is a terrestrial planet with a rocky composition.
   Mars has a solid surface covered by rocks, dust, craters, valleys, and plains.
   Mars is commonly called the Red Planet.
   Its reddish appearance is mainly caused by iron minerals that have oxidized.
   Mars has a thin atmosphere compared with Earth.
   Carbon dioxide is the dominant gas in the Martian atmosphere.
   Mars has two small natural satellites, Phobos and Deimos.
   Mars has polar ice caps containing water ice and frozen carbon dioxide.
   Mars has the largest volcano and one of the largest canyon systems known in the Solar System.
   Mars has evidence of ancient geological environments involving liquid water.
   Mars is one of the most extensively explored planets beyond Earth.
   Mars is a major target in the search for past or present extraterrestrial life.

2. BASIC PROPERTIES
   Mars has a diameter of approximately 6,779 kilometers.
   Mars has about half the diameter of Earth.
   Mars has about 11 percent of Earth's mass.
   Mars has surface gravity about 38 percent as strong as Earth's.
   Mars is less dense than Earth.
   Mars has an average distance from the Sun of about 228 million kilometers.
   Mars receives less sunlight than Earth because it is farther from the Sun.
   Mars completes one orbit around the Sun in about 687 Earth days.
   A Martian solar day is called a sol.
   One sol lasts approximately 24 hours and 39 minutes.
   Mars has an axial tilt of about 25 degrees.
   Its axial tilt produces seasons similar in principle to Earth's seasons.

3. FORMATION
   Mars formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago.
   Mars formed from material in the early Solar System.
   The planet developed through accretion of rocky and metallic material.
   Mars formed in the inner Solar System.
   Its interior became differentiated into a core, mantle, and crust.
   Mars underwent significant volcanic and geological activity during its early history.
   The planet gradually lost much of its early internal heat.
   Mars became less geologically active than Earth.
   Its atmosphere and surface environment changed substantially over geological time.
   Studying Mars helps scientists understand the evolution of terrestrial planets.

4. ORBIT
   Mars follows an elliptical orbit around the Sun.
   Mars has a more eccentric orbit than Earth.
   The average distance between Mars and the Sun is about 228 million kilometers.
   Mars completes one revolution around the Sun in approximately 687 Earth days.
   Mars travels around the Sun at an average speed of about 24 kilometers per second.
   The distance between Mars and the Sun changes significantly during its orbit.
   Mars reaches perihelion when it is closest to the Sun.
   Mars reaches aphelion when it is farthest from the Sun.
   The eccentric orbit contributes to differences in seasonal duration and intensity.
   Mars and Earth periodically come relatively close to each other.
   These close approaches are important for planning spacecraft missions.

5. ROTATION
   Mars rotates around its axis in the same general direction as Earth.
   A Martian solar day is called a sol.
   One sol lasts approximately 24 hours and 39 minutes.
   Mars completes a rotation relative to distant stars in about 24 hours and 37 minutes.
   Mars has a rotation period similar to Earth's.
   The similar rotation period makes Mars useful for studying daily cycles.
   Mars's axial tilt is approximately 25 degrees.
   The axial tilt produces seasonal changes.
   Seasonal changes affect temperature, atmospheric circulation, and polar ice.
   Dust activity can also vary with Martian seasons.

6. ATMOSPHERE
   Mars has a very thin atmosphere.
   Carbon dioxide makes up most of the Martian atmosphere.
   Nitrogen and argon are present in smaller amounts.
   Water vapor exists in the Martian atmosphere in small quantities.
   The atmospheric pressure at the surface is less than one percent of Earth's average sea-level pressure.
   The thin atmosphere provides little insulation.
   Surface temperatures can vary strongly between day and night.
   The atmosphere contains suspended dust particles.
   Dust can be lifted from the surface by winds.
   Large regional or global dust storms can occur.
   The thin atmosphere makes liquid water unstable on much of the present surface.
   Atmospheric conditions have changed significantly throughout Martian history.

7. TEMPERATURE
   Mars is much colder on average than Earth.
   Surface temperatures vary strongly with location, season, and time of day.
   Temperatures can fall to extremely low values during Martian nights.
   Equatorial regions can become relatively warm during daytime.
   The thin atmosphere contributes to rapid temperature changes.
   Polar regions become especially cold during winter.
   Carbon dioxide can freeze at the Martian poles during seasonal changes.
   Temperature variations influence surface frost and atmospheric circulation.
   Understanding Martian temperatures is important for spacecraft and future human missions.

8. RED SURFACE
   Mars appears red when viewed from space and from Earth.
   The red color is associated with oxidized iron-bearing minerals.
   Fine dust containing iron minerals covers much of the Martian surface.
   Winds transport dust across the planet.
   Dust can settle on rocks, plains, and spacecraft.
   The reddish dust gives Mars its distinctive appearance.
   Surface colors vary depending on rock type, dust coverage, and lighting conditions.
   The composition of Martian dust provides information about surface chemistry.
   Studying iron minerals helps scientists understand the planet's geological and atmospheric history.

9. SURFACE GEOLOGY
   Mars contains impact craters, volcanoes, canyons, dunes, plains, and valleys.
   The northern hemisphere contains extensive low-lying plains.
   The southern hemisphere contains older and more heavily cratered terrain.
   The boundary between major northern and southern terrains is called the Martian dichotomy.
   Mars has ancient geological surfaces that preserve evidence of early planetary conditions.
   Volcanism shaped large parts of the planet.
   Wind erosion and deposition continue to modify the surface.
   Impacts have produced craters throughout Martian history.
   Water-related processes also modified parts of the surface in the past.
   Mars therefore contains evidence of multiple geological processes.

10. OLYMPUS MONS
    Olympus Mons is a giant shield volcano on Mars.
    Olympus Mons is the largest known volcano in the Solar System.
    It rises approximately 22 kilometers above the surrounding plains.
    Olympus Mons is much wider than typical terrestrial volcanoes.
    Its broad shape is characteristic of a shield volcano.
    Low surface gravity allowed very large volcanic structures to develop.
    Repeated lava flows built Olympus Mons over a long period.
    The volcano is located in the Tharsis region.
    Olympus Mons provides evidence of extensive volcanic activity on ancient Mars.

11. VALLES MARINERIS
    Valles Marineris is a vast canyon system on Mars.
    It extends for thousands of kilometers across the Martian surface.
    The canyon system is located mainly near the equator.
    Valles Marineris is much larger than the Grand Canyon on Earth.
    The system includes deep valleys, cliffs, and interconnected troughs.
    Its formation is associated mainly with tectonic processes and later erosion.
    Water and other surface processes may have modified parts of the canyon system.
    The canyon system provides evidence of Mars's complex geological history.
    Valles Marineris is one of the most prominent features visible on Mars.

12. WATER
    Mars contains water mainly in the form of ice.
    Large quantities of water ice exist in the polar regions.
    Water ice also occurs beneath the surface in many locations.
    Evidence shows that liquid water flowed across parts of Mars in the distant past.
    Ancient river channels are visible on the Martian surface.
    Ancient lake basins have been identified.
    Minerals formed in the presence of water have been detected.
    Some Martian rocks contain evidence of past interaction with water.
    Present-day surface conditions generally do not allow stable liquid water for long periods.
    The history of Martian water is central to studies of habitability.

13. ANCIENT RIVERS AND LAKES
    Mars has many geological features interpreted as ancient river channels.
    Some valleys appear to have been carved by flowing water.
    Ancient lake environments existed in several Martian regions.
    Sedimentary rocks can preserve evidence of past water activity.
    Water may have been more abundant on Mars early in its history.
    The ancient atmosphere may have been thicker than the present atmosphere.
    The climate of early Mars is an active research topic.
    Water-related minerals provide additional evidence for ancient aqueous environments.
    Studying ancient water systems helps determine whether Mars once had habitable environments.

14. POLAR CAPS
    Mars has permanent polar deposits.
    The polar regions contain substantial water ice.
    Seasonal carbon dioxide frost expands and contracts around the poles.
    During winter, carbon dioxide can freeze onto the polar surface.
    During warmer seasons, some seasonal carbon dioxide ice returns to the atmosphere.
    The polar caps influence atmospheric pressure over seasonal cycles.
    Polar layers preserve information about changes in Martian climate.
    Radar observations have been used to study subsurface polar materials.
    The polar regions are important targets for understanding Martian climate history.

15. DUST AND WEATHER
    Mars experiences frequent dust activity.
    Dust devils can lift fine particles from the surface.
    Dust devils can leave tracks on the ground.
    Regional dust storms can grow over large areas.
    Some dust storms can become global.
    Dust in the atmosphere reduces visibility and affects solar energy reaching the surface.
    Dust can settle on spacecraft surfaces.
    Martian winds transport and redistribute fine material.
    Atmospheric dust also influences temperature by interacting with sunlight and infrared radiation.
    Dust activity is an important part of Martian weather.

16. MAGNETIC FIELD AND ATMOSPHERIC LOSS
    Mars does not currently have a strong global magnetic field like Earth.
    Some regions of the Martian crust retain evidence of ancient magnetization.
    This suggests Mars may have had a stronger global magnetic field early in its history.
    The loss of a global magnetic field allowed the solar wind to interact more directly with the upper atmosphere.
    Solar wind interactions can contribute to atmospheric escape.
    Atmospheric loss is one factor in the long-term evolution of Mars.
    Mars's thin present atmosphere may partly result from billions of years of atmospheric loss.
    Studying atmospheric escape helps explain how Mars changed from its ancient environment to its present state.

17. MOONS
    Mars has two known natural satellites.
    Phobos is the larger and closer moon of Mars.
    Deimos is smaller and farther from Mars than Phobos.
    Both moons are irregularly shaped.
    Phobos orbits Mars much closer than Earth's Moon orbits Earth.
    Phobos completes an orbit around Mars faster than Mars rotates.
    Deimos has a slower orbital period than Phobos.
    The origins of Phobos and Deimos are still debated.
    Their composition and orbital properties provide clues about their formation.
    Both moons have been studied by spacecraft and remote observations.

18. EXPLORATION
    Mars has been explored by orbiters, landers, and rovers.
    Mariner 4 returned the first close-up images of Mars in 1965.
    Viking 1 and Viking 2 successfully landed on Mars in 1976.
    Viking landers performed experiments to study the Martian soil and environment.
    Mars Pathfinder demonstrated rover operations on the Martian surface in 1997.
    Spirit and Opportunity were NASA rovers that explored Mars for extended periods.
    Curiosity has studied Martian geology and environmental history since landing in 2012.
    Perseverance landed in Jezero Crater in 2021.
    Perseverance is studying rocks and ancient environments associated with a former lake basin.
    Ingenuity demonstrated powered flight on another planet.
    Mars exploration has greatly increased knowledge of the planet's geology and atmosphere.

19. HABITABILITY
    Habitability refers to conditions that could support life.
    Mars is not currently known to support life.
    Ancient Mars may have had environments suitable for microbial life.
    Liquid water existed on parts of the surface in the distant past.
    Some minerals found on Mars formed in aqueous environments.
    Organic molecules have been detected in Martian materials.
    Organic molecules are carbon-containing compounds and do not by themselves prove life.
    Mars missions search for evidence of past habitability and possible biosignatures.
    The preservation of ancient rocks makes Mars important for astrobiology.
    Determining whether life ever existed on Mars remains an open scientific question.

20. KEY TERMS
    Mars: The fourth planet from the Sun and a rocky terrestrial planet.
    Red Planet: A common name for Mars based on its reddish appearance.
    Sol: One Martian solar day, lasting about 24 hours and 39 minutes.
    Perihelion: The point in Mars's orbit closest to the Sun.
    Aphelion: The point in Mars's orbit farthest from the Sun.
    Regolith: Loose material covering the Martian surface.
    Olympus Mons: The largest known volcano in the Solar System.
    Valles Marineris: A giant canyon system extending thousands of kilometers across Mars.
    Tharsis: A large volcanic region on Mars.
    Dust devil: A rotating column of air that lifts dust from the Martian surface.
    Atmospheric escape: The loss of atmospheric particles into space.
    Habitability: The potential of an environment to support life.
    Biosignature: A feature or substance that may provide evidence of biological activity.
    Phobos: The larger and closer moon of Mars.
    Deimos: The smaller and more distant moon of Mars.
    Jezero Crater: A Martian crater investigated by the Perseverance rover.
