JUPITER

1. OVERVIEW
   Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun.
   Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System.
   Jupiter is a gas giant.
   Jupiter is composed primarily of hydrogen and helium.
   Jupiter does not have a solid surface like Earth.
   The visible cloud tops form the upper part of Jupiter's atmosphere.
   Jupiter has a powerful magnetic field.
   Jupiter has a large system of natural satellites.
   Jupiter has a faint ring system.
   Jupiter rotates faster than any other planet in the Solar System.
   One Jupiter rotation takes about 10 hours.
   Jupiter takes approximately 12 Earth years to orbit the Sun.
   Jupiter has a mass more than 300 times that of Earth.
   Jupiter is about 11 times wider than Earth.
   Jupiter plays an important role in studies of giant planet formation and evolution.

2. BASIC PROPERTIES
   Jupiter has a diameter of approximately 139,820 kilometers.
   Jupiter has an average distance from the Sun of about 778 million kilometers.
   Jupiter receives much less sunlight than Earth because of its greater distance from the Sun.
   Jupiter has an average orbital speed of about 13 kilometers per second.
   Jupiter's rapid rotation causes a noticeable equatorial bulge.
   Jupiter's equatorial diameter is larger than its polar diameter.
   Jupiter's density is lower than Earth's because it contains large amounts of hydrogen and helium.
   Jupiter's enormous mass produces strong surface-level gravity at its cloud tops.
   Jupiter has no clearly defined solid surface.
   Pressure and temperature increase continuously with depth.

3. FORMATION
   Jupiter formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago.
   Jupiter formed from material in the early Solar System.
   The planet formed in the colder outer region of the protoplanetary disk.
   Hydrogen and helium were abundant in the early Solar System.
   Jupiter accumulated a large amount of gas during its formation.
   Its large mass allowed it to retain substantial hydrogen and helium.
   The exact details of Jupiter's formation are still studied.
   Models of Jupiter's formation help explain the structure of the Solar System.
   Jupiter's composition provides evidence about conditions in the early planetary system.

4. ORBIT
   Jupiter follows an elliptical orbit around the Sun.
   Jupiter's average orbital distance is approximately 778 million kilometers.
   Jupiter completes one orbit in about 11.86 Earth years.
   Jupiter travels around the Sun at an average speed of approximately 13 kilometers per second.
   Jupiter's orbit is farther from the Sun than those of the terrestrial planets.
   Jupiter's orbital period is much longer than Earth's.
   The planet's large mass influences the orbits of many smaller Solar System bodies.
   Jupiter's gravitational influence affects asteroids, comets, and other planets.
   Jupiter is sometimes described as an important gravitational perturber in the Solar System.

5. ROTATION
   Jupiter rotates extremely rapidly.
   One rotation takes approximately 9 hours and 56 minutes.
   Jupiter has the shortest rotation period of all eight planets.
   Rapid rotation causes Jupiter to have a pronounced equatorial bulge.
   Jupiter's atmosphere rotates at different speeds depending on latitude.
   This difference is called differential rotation.
   The rapid rotation contributes to the formation of strong atmospheric bands.
   Jupiter's rotation influences its powerful weather systems.
   The rapid spin is important for understanding Jupiter's atmospheric dynamics.

6. ATMOSPHERE
   Jupiter's atmosphere is composed mainly of hydrogen and helium.
   Small quantities of methane, ammonia, water vapor, and other compounds are also present.
   The upper atmosphere contains thick layers of clouds.
   Ammonia ice clouds occur in the upper cloud layers.
   Deeper clouds contain other compounds formed under higher pressure and temperature.
   Jupiter's atmosphere has alternating light and dark bands.
   These bands extend around the planet.
   The bands are associated with strong east-west winds.
   Jupiter has powerful storms and atmospheric vortices.
   The atmosphere becomes denser and hotter with increasing depth.

7. CLOUD BANDS
   Jupiter's visible atmosphere contains zones and belts.
   Zones are generally lighter-colored regions.
   Belts are generally darker-colored regions.
   Strong winds occur between adjacent atmospheric bands.
   The winds can reach several hundred kilometers per hour.
   Different bands can move in different directions.
   The boundaries between bands can contain turbulent weather.
   The banded structure results from Jupiter's rapid rotation and atmospheric dynamics.
   Cloud colors can be influenced by chemical compounds and photochemical reactions.
   The exact causes of some color variations remain under investigation.

8. GREAT RED SPOT
   The Great Red Spot is a giant atmospheric storm on Jupiter.
   It is located in Jupiter's southern hemisphere.
   The Great Red Spot is an anticyclonic storm.
   An anticyclone rotates in the opposite sense to a cyclone in the same hemisphere.
   The storm has persisted for centuries of recorded observations.
   Its exact origin and long-term evolution are active research topics.
   The Great Red Spot has changed in size and shape over time.
   Its reddish color may result from complex atmospheric chemistry.
   Winds around the storm are extremely strong.
   The Great Red Spot is one of Jupiter's most recognizable features.

9. STORMS AND WEATHER
   Jupiter has some of the most powerful weather systems in the Solar System.
   Large storms can form and interact within the atmosphere.
   Lightning has been observed in Jupiter's clouds.
   Water is believed to play an important role in deeper atmospheric processes.
   Storms can transport heat and material vertically.
   Atmospheric turbulence creates complex cloud patterns.
   Jet streams separate many of Jupiter's major cloud bands.
   Weather systems can persist for long periods.
   Jupiter's lack of a solid surface allows atmospheric circulation to behave differently from Earth's.
   Studying Jupiter's weather helps scientists understand giant-planet atmospheres.

10. INTERNAL STRUCTURE
    Jupiter does not have a solid surface.
    The atmosphere gradually becomes denser with depth.
    Hydrogen changes into exotic high-pressure states deeper inside the planet.
    At sufficiently high pressures, hydrogen can become metallic.
    Metallic hydrogen can conduct electricity.
    The metallic hydrogen region is associated with Jupiter's powerful magnetic field.
    Jupiter may contain a dense central region or diluted core.
    The exact structure of Jupiter's deep interior remains an active research area.
    Pressure and temperature increase enormously toward the center.
    Jupiter's interior stores a large amount of thermal energy.

11. MAGNETIC FIELD
    Jupiter has the strongest planetary magnetic field in the Solar System.
    Its magnetosphere is enormous.
    The magnetosphere extends millions of kilometers into space on the side facing away from the Sun.
    Jupiter's magnetic field is generated by electrically conducting material deep inside the planet.
    Metallic hydrogen is believed to contribute to the magnetic field.
    The magnetic field traps charged particles around Jupiter.
    Jupiter's magnetosphere interacts strongly with the solar wind.
    The magnetic environment produces intense radiation belts.
    Jupiter's magnetic field also interacts with its moons.

12. RADIATION
    Jupiter has extremely intense radiation belts.
    The radiation is produced by energetic charged particles trapped by its magnetic field.
    The radiation environment near Jupiter can be hazardous to spacecraft.
    Io contributes material to Jupiter's magnetosphere through volcanic activity.
    Charged particles can become trapped and accelerated around Jupiter.
    The radiation environment varies with location.
    Spacecraft designed to study Jupiter require radiation-resistant systems.
    Understanding Jupiter's radiation belts is important for mission planning.

13. RINGS
    Jupiter has a faint ring system.
    The rings are much less prominent than Saturn's rings.
    Jupiter's rings are composed mainly of fine dust particles.
    Much of the ring material is believed to originate from impacts on small moons.
    The main ring is surrounded by fainter ring structures.
    Jupiter's rings are difficult to observe from Earth.
    Spacecraft have provided detailed observations of the ring system.
    The rings demonstrate that ring systems are not unique to Saturn.

14. MOONS
    Jupiter has a large number of known natural satellites.
    The four largest moons are Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto.
    These four moons are called the Galilean moons.
    Galileo Galilei observed the four large moons in 1610.
    Io is the most volcanically active world known in the Solar System.
    Europa has a thick layer of ice covering a likely global subsurface ocean.
    Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System.
    Ganymede has its own intrinsic magnetic field.
    Callisto is heavily cratered and has a relatively ancient surface.
    The Galilean moons are major targets for planetary science.

15. IO
    Io is the innermost of the four Galilean moons.
    Io is slightly larger than Earth's Moon.
    Io is the most volcanically active world known.
    Its volcanic activity is driven mainly by tidal heating.
    Jupiter's gravity and interactions with other moons produce strong tidal stresses.
    Io has numerous active volcanoes.
    Volcanic eruptions release sulfur compounds and other materials.
    Io's surface is covered by volcanic plains and deposits.
    Io contributes material to Jupiter's surrounding plasma environment.
    Studying Io helps scientists understand extreme tidal heating.

16. EUROPA
    Europa is one of Jupiter's four Galilean moons.
    Europa has a surface dominated by water ice.
    The surface contains cracks, ridges, and relatively few large impact craters.
    Evidence strongly suggests that a salty ocean exists beneath Europa's ice shell.
    The subsurface ocean makes Europa an important astrobiological target.
    Tidal heating may help maintain liquid water beneath the ice.
    Europa's ocean may contain conditions relevant to habitability.
    The thickness and structure of the ice shell are active research topics.
    Europa Clipper is a NASA mission designed to investigate Europa.
    Europa is one of the most important ocean worlds in the Solar System.

17. GANYMEDE
    Ganymede is the largest natural satellite in the Solar System.
    Ganymede is larger in diameter than Mercury.
    Ganymede is composed mainly of rock and water ice.
    Ganymede has a differentiated internal structure.
    It possesses an intrinsic magnetic field.
    Ganymede is the only moon known to have its own internally generated magnetic field.
    Evidence indicates that Ganymede contains a deep subsurface ocean.
    Its surface includes both dark and bright terrains.
    Ganymede has been studied by several spacecraft.
    The JUICE mission is designed to investigate Ganymede and other Jovian moons.

18. CALLISTO
    Callisto is the outermost of the four Galilean moons.
    Callisto is heavily covered with impact craters.
    Its surface is among the oldest and most heavily cratered in the Solar System.
    Callisto is composed primarily of rock and water ice.
    Evidence suggests that a subsurface ocean may exist beneath its surface.
    Callisto lies outside Jupiter's main radiation belts compared with the inner Galilean moons.
    Its lower radiation environment makes it scientifically interesting for future exploration.
    Callisto's ancient surface preserves evidence of the early Solar System.
    The moon provides a useful comparison with Io, Europa, and Ganymede.

19. GRAVITATIONAL INFLUENCE
    Jupiter has enormous gravitational influence because of its large mass.
    Its gravity affects the trajectories of asteroids and comets.
    Jupiter can alter the orbital paths of small Solar System bodies.
    Its gravity contributes to the structure of the asteroid populations.
    Jupiter's moons are strongly controlled by its gravitational field.
    Jupiter's gravitational interactions can sometimes send objects toward the inner Solar System.
    Jupiter can also eject some small bodies into different orbits or out of the Solar System.
    Its gravitational role is important in models of Solar System evolution.
    Jupiter is therefore important beyond its own physical environment.

20. EXPLORATION
    Jupiter has been visited by multiple robotic spacecraft.
    Pioneer 10 was the first spacecraft to fly past Jupiter.
    Pioneer 11 also conducted a Jupiter flyby.
    Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 provided detailed observations of Jupiter and its moons.
    Galileo became the first spacecraft to orbit Jupiter.
    Galileo studied Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetosphere, and major moons.
    Juno entered orbit around Jupiter in 2016.
    Juno has investigated Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field, gravity field, and interior.
    Juno observations have improved understanding of Jupiter's deep atmospheric structure.
    Future missions continue to investigate Jupiter's moons and environment.

21. SCIENTIFIC IMPORTANCE
    Jupiter is a key example of a gas giant planet.
    Its enormous size makes it important for studying giant-planet formation.
    Its atmosphere contains powerful storms and complex circulation.
    Its magnetic field creates an extreme magnetospheric environment.
    Its large moon system contains worlds with very different geological properties.
    Europa and Ganymede are important targets in the search for potentially habitable environments.
    Jupiter's gravity influences many smaller Solar System objects.
    The planet provides a natural laboratory for studying hydrogen under extreme pressure.
    Understanding Jupiter helps scientists interpret giant exoplanets around other stars.

22. KEY TERMS
    Jupiter: The largest planet in the Solar System.
    Gas giant: A large planet dominated by hydrogen and helium.
    Great Red Spot: A long-lived anticyclonic storm in Jupiter's atmosphere.
    Magnetosphere: The region controlled by Jupiter's magnetic field.
    Metallic hydrogen: A high-pressure conducting state of hydrogen expected deep inside Jupiter.
    Differential rotation: Rotation in which different atmospheric latitudes move at different speeds.
    Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto.
    Io: Jupiter's volcanically active inner Galilean moon.
    Europa: An icy Galilean moon believed to contain a subsurface ocean.
    Ganymede: The largest moon in the Solar System.
    Callisto: A heavily cratered outer Galilean moon.
    Tidal heating: Internal heating produced by gravitational deformation.
    Solar wind: A stream of charged particles flowing outward from the Sun.
